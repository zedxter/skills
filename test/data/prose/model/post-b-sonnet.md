Two years ago, I shut down [Startup Name].

We raised [$X], built a team of [N], and spent [X months] chasing a problem I was sure the world needed solved. It didn't. Or at least, not enough people needed it enough to pay.

Here's what I wish I'd known at the start:

**1. I fell in love with the solution, not the problem.**
I spent months building before I had a single paying customer. Compliments felt like validation. They weren't. The only real signal is someone pulling out their wallet.

**2. "Nice to have" is a slow death.**
Customers told me they loved the product, then never opened it again. If it's not painful to go without, it's not a business.

**3. I hired too fast and talked too little.**
Growing the team felt like progress. In reality, I was scaling my own uncertainty. A small team that talks to customers daily beats a big team guessing.

**4. I waited too long to face the numbers.**
Runway doesn't shrink gradually. It disappears. I should have set kill criteria on day one: "If we don't hit X by month Y, we change course or stop."

**5. Co-founder alignment matters more than the idea.**
Disagreements we ignored early became the ones that broke us later. Have the uncomfortable conversations before they're expensive.

**6. Failure didn't destroy me, but hiding from it would have.**
The hardest part wasn't the shutdown. It was the shame. Once I started talking about it openly, people shared their own stories, and doors I didn't expect began to open.
