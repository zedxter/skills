Some personal news: I just accepted my first job as a software engineer! 🎉

A year ago, I was in a career that left me feeling stuck. I kept wondering whether I could actually build things for a living, so I took a leap and enrolled in a coding bootcamp.

The bootcamp was intense. Late nights, bugs I couldn't solve for hours, and plenty of moments of "Do I really belong here?" Imposter syndrome showed up early and stayed for a while.

Then came the job search, which was harder than the bootcamp. Here's what it looked like:

• Dozens of applications with no response
• Rejections that stung, even the polite ones
• Technical interviews where my mind went blank
• Weeks of wondering if I'd made the wrong call

What kept me going:

1. Building real projects, not just tutorials. Having something to show made every conversation easier.
2. Reaching out to people. Most of my best leads came from conversations, not job boards.
3. Treating every rejection as practice. Each interview made me a little sharper.
4. My cohort. Having people who understood the grind made all the difference.

And then one day, an offer came. I'm excited to join [Company] as a [Role], where I'll be working on [what you'll be working on].

To my instructors, mentors, classmates, and everyone who reviewed my résumé, did a mock interview, or just told me to keep going: thank you. I couldn't have done it without you.

To anyone in the middle of the struggle right now: it's not a straight line, but it's possible. Keep building. Keep applying. Your moment is coming.

This is just the beginning, and I can't wait to learn, grow, and contribute. 🚀
