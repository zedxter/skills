I start on Monday as a junior software engineer, and I still find that sentence strange to type.

Eleven months ago I was a project coordinator at a logistics company. I was fine at it, but I spent most evenings building small things in a browser tab, like a script that renamed my files and a budget tracker that never worked. In February I quit and joined a bootcamp. My partner and I did the arithmetic on our savings twice, and then a third time, and then I signed up anyway.

The bootcamp lasted sixteen weeks. About half of what I learned I have never used since, and the other half I use every day. I couldn't have told you in advance which half would be which. What helped most was the unglamorous stuff: reading error messages slowly, learning to write a decent commit message, and sitting through code review without taking it personally. That last one took me about two months.

The search took longer than the course. I sent 140 applications and got nine interviews. I failed a take-home test because I spent six hours polishing the wrong part of it. I froze in a live coding round and could not remember how to loop over an array, something I had done a thousand times. I got one offer, from a company of about forty people that builds scheduling software for clinics. The engineer who interviewed me asked what I had gotten stuck on lately and how I got unstuck. I told him about a bug I had chased for two days that turned out to be a timezone issue, and we spent twenty minutes comparing timezone horror stories. I think that conversation got me the job.

My old coworkers were kinder about all this than I expected. One of them, Marta, kept sending me job postings she saw, including several I was badly underqualified for. I applied to one of them anyway, and it went nowhere, but I appreciated it.

I don't know yet whether I'll be good at this. I know the first week will mostly involve me nodding at things I don't follow and writing them down to look up later. I'm okay with that.

If you're hiring juniors, thank you. If you're a junior who is still searching, I'm sorry it's slow right now, and I'm happy to look at your portfolio or trade interview stories if that would be useful.
