Two years ago I shut down a company I'd spent nineteen months building. It was software for independent physiotherapy clinics: scheduling, reminders, billing. We had eleven paying clinics at the peak and a lot of confidence about the other four thousand.

The product worked. People who used it said nice things. That turned out to be the problem, because I mistook nice things for need. When I asked clinic owners if they'd be upset if the software disappeared, most said they'd be a little annoyed. I wrote that down as a positive signal. A little annoyed is what you feel when your pen runs dry.

I spent the first eight months building before I'd charged anyone a cent. I told myself it was research. It was actually avoidance. Asking for money is a small, uncomfortable conversation, and building is a comfortable way to postpone it. When I finally put a price on the product, three of the first five clinics said yes immediately, and I should have learned something from how fast they said it. They were the ones who already had a spreadsheet they hated. The other two were curious, and curiosity doesn't renew every month.

I also hired too early. Two good people joined when I had no proof the thing would grow, and the payroll made every decision feel urgent. Urgent decisions are mostly bad ones. I added features for a clinic that was never going to pay more, because losing it felt like the end of the world. It wasn't, and neither was it going to save us.

What I'd do differently is boring. Charge from the first week, even if the number is embarrassing. Talk to the people who said no and ask them what they use instead, because the answer describes your real competitor. Keep the team small until the revenue argues for something else.

I think about the clinic in Leeds a lot. The owner emailed me after I announced the shutdown and said she'd had to go back to paper. She wasn't angry. She just said it had been easier with the software. That one sentence told me more than all the earlier compliments, and I had to lose the company to get it.
