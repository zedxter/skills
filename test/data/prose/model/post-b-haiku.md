I failed at building a startup. Here's what it taught me.

Nine months in, we had a product people said they liked, a waitlist that kept growing, and a bank balance that was shrinking faster than our traction. We shut it down last month.

Here's what I'd tell anyone starting out:

**1. "People like it" is not demand.** Compliments cost nothing. We kept hearing "this is cool" and mistook politeness for pull. The real test was whether anyone paid, or whether they came back without being asked.

**2. Build the smallest version that tests your riskiest assumption.** We spent four months on features before checking whether the core problem was painful enough for people to change their behavior. It wasn't, at least not for the customers we were targeting.

**3. Talk to people who said no.** The customers who churned or never signed up told us more than the ones who loved us. Their objections were the roadmap we needed.

**4. Runway is a decision-making tool.** When cash gets tight, every choice gets more expensive. I wish I'd set a hard checkpoint ("if we don't hit X by month Y, we stop or pivot") earlier, instead of letting hope set the timeline.

**5. Failing isn't the same as being a failure.** I learned more in these months than in several years of employment. The skills, the network, and the clarity about what I want next are all real.

If you're in the middle of something that isn't working, I'd gently suggest asking yourself the hard question now, while you still have options.
